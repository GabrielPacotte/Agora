# Agora — Vision

## Why Agora Exists

Agora was born from a simple observation:  
most existing discussion platforms are not designed to foster understanding.

They optimize for attention, speed, and emotional reaction rather than for clarity, nuance, or collective reasoning.  
Public debate becomes fragmented, polarized, or reduced to performance.

Agora does not aim to replace existing social networks.  
Its goal is to explore **a different kind of digital space**, focused on structured discussion, transparency, and long-term sustainability.

---

## A Modern Agora

In ancient Greece, the agora was a public place:
- open to all citizens,
- structured by shared rules,
- where ideas could be confronted openly,
- and where debate was part of civic life.

Agora seeks to reinterpret this idea in a modern digital context, while acknowledging today’s constraints:
- scale,
- anonymity,
- abuse risks,
- technical complexity.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Debate over performance

Agora is not built to maximize engagement or emotional impact.  
It prioritizes:
- clarity of ideas,
- explicit positions,
- readable and contextualized discussions.

A post is not a punchline.  
It is an invitation to debate.

---

### 2. Structure as a tool for thinking

Discussions on Agora are intentionally structured:
- posts expose multiple stances,
- comments are contextualized,
- content is clearly separated from social signals.

Structure is not a restriction.  
It is a **cognitive tool** meant to support reasoning and nuance.

---

### 3. Responsibility without mass surveillance

Agora aims to protect anonymity while discouraging abuse.

The project explicitly rejects mass surveillance and opaque data collection.  
At the same time, it acknowledges that a discussion space must remain viable and protected against severe misuse.

How this balance is achieved is an **open and evolving question**, and part of the long-term research and design of the project.

---

### 4. Moderation as a future challenge, not a shortcut

Moderation is not treated as an afterthought, but it is also not rushed.

Agora does not rely on automated or purely algorithmic moderation.  
Instead, it treats moderation as a complex human process that must be carefully designed, documented, and auditable.

At the current stage, moderation is intentionally limited.  
More advanced mechanisms will only be introduced once their implications are fully understood.

---

### 5. Durability over virality

Agora is designed to last.

This implies:
- a clear and explicit architecture,
- strong separation of responsibilities,
- conservative technical choices,
- slow and controlled evolution.

Growth is not a primary objective.  
Consistency and maintainability are.

---

## An Open-Source Project with High Standards

Agora is open-source by choice and by conviction.

The project embraces:
- strict code quality requirements,
- extensive documentation,
- a clear architectural vision.

Contributing to Agora requires care, time, and respect for the existing design.  
This is not a project optimized for quick or superficial contributions.

---

## A Digital Common

Agora is not a startup in disguise.  
It is not designed to be sold, monetized, or captured.

It is closer in spirit to a **digital common**, similar to projects like Wikipedia:
- forkable,
- self-hostable,
- adaptable to different communities.

Its success is not measured by user count, but by the quality of the discussions it enables.

---

## What Agora Is Not

- A mainstream social network
- A content recommendation engine
- A personal branding platform
- A shortcut to solving online discourse

Agora is a framework.  
What communities build with it is up to them.

---

## An Evolving Vision

This vision is not fixed.

It is expected to evolve through:
- real usage,
- technical experimentation,
- contributor discussions,
- and critical feedback.

However, any evolution should remain aligned with the original intent:
**to create a digital discussion space worthy of serious thought.**

---

## Closing Words

Agora does not aim to be popular.  
It aims to be honest.

If it finds its audience, that is a success.  
If it remains a niche but meaningful tool, that is also a success.
